Fable 5 is only included in subscription quota through July 7, which makes the real question less about raw model quality and more about workflow design: where does the expensive model actually matter?

I wrote a new Inside ClaudeKit note after reading two strong X posts from Thariq and Alex Finn, then mapping their insights into a practical ClaudeKit workflow.

The core idea is to find unknowns before coding, not after the implementation has already hardened around the wrong assumptions.

The workflow has 6 stages and 3 custom skills layered on top of ClaudeKit:

• `blindspot` runs before planning to turn vague uncertainty into concrete unknowns, risk areas, and sharper prompts.
• `impl-notes` runs during implementation to record deviations from the plan while they happen.
• `quiz-gate` runs after code review to check whether the human actually understands the behavior that just shipped.

The post also covers model cost tradeoffs: where Fable 5 is worth using, where Sonnet/Haiku is enough, why Codex should be a cross-model gate rather than an automatic review loop, and how built-in Explore subagent behavior can accidentally burn premium usage.
